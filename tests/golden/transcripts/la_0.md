## Q: Compare LA and Santa Ana airport congestion levels.

### Direct answer

**LAX is more congested than Santa Ana (SNA), but not by much.** On the constraint index C, where 0 is the least constrained US airport and 1 is the most, LAX scores **0.80** (range 0.79–0.81) and John Wayne/Orange County scores **0.75** (range 0.74–0.75). The two ranges don't overlap, so LAX's lead is consistent. Both airports sit in the upper part of the US distribution.

| Signal | LAX (large hub) | SNA (medium hub) |
|---|---|---|
| **C (constraint index)** | **0.80** (0.79–0.81) | **0.75** (0.74–0.75) |
| Load factor | 82.2% (higher than 92% of US airports) | 79.7% (higher than 74%) |
| Peak saturation | 93.8% (higher than 58%) | 88.1% (higher than 42%) |
| Taxi-out (runway queue) | +3.5 min (higher than 82%) | +6.2 min (higher than 95%) |
| Taxi-in (waiting for a gate) | +2.8 min (higher than 88%) | +2.6 min (higher than 87%) |
| Delay rate | 20.2% | 20.2% |
| Cancellation rate | 0.9% | 1.1% |

### What drives the difference

- **LAX: congestion comes from demand.** Planes are fuller than at 92% of US airports, and peak-hour saturation is higher. The airport is busy across the board, from seats to schedule.
- **SNA: congestion comes from the runway.** SNA's runway queue (+6.2 min) is among the worst in the country, higher than 95% of US airports. Its load factor and peak saturation are lower than LAX's.
- **Gate waits are nearly the same** at both, around the high-80s percentile. Delay rates are identical at 20.2%, and SNA cancels slightly more often.

### What this means for a renovation investment

At **LAX**, terminal and gate capacity projects line up with the main bottleneck. Full planes and high peak saturation point to demand pressing against existing facilities.

At **SNA**, the biggest pain point is on the airfield (runway queues) rather than in the terminal. A terminal or gate renovation would address the gate-wait signal, but not the main constraint. Weigh whether a terminal project would actually relieve the congestion that matters most there.

### Assumptions & uncertainty

- **Data period:** Aug 2025 – Jul 2026.
- **Sources:** Constraint signals come from BTS on-time data, covering domestic flights of reporting airlines only. Growth inputs come from the FAA Terminal Area Forecast (TAF), and forecasts can be wrong. Airports the FAA doesn't forecast use a fallback growth of 2% a year.
- **Ranges:** C is 0.79–0.81 for LAX and 0.74–0.75 for SNA.
- **Caveat (both airports):** The growth range isn't available yet, so only the constraint part of the range is measured.
- **What C is:** C ranks how constrained an airport is compared with other US airports. The Renovation Profitability Score (RPS) is a ranking index, not a passenger forecast. No RPS values were provided for this comparison.
- **What the data can't show:**
  - Demand turned away today can't be counted from public data. Full planes count only through load factor.
  - Gate shortage is measured indirectly, from planes waiting for a gate after landing.
  - RPS doesn't include construction cost, so it is not an IRR.
- **Scope notes:** None provided. No forecast horizon was specified.

This is a screening signal, not investment advice.
