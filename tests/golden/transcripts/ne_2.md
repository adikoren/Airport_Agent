### What changes versus the 5-year view

- **BOS's lead is now secure.** Its range (15.6–16.4) no longer overlaps BTV's (11.0–13.3). BOS also adds by far the most passengers: 5.79M on a 20.95M base.
  - The earlier caveat still applies. BOS's pressure is mainly at the runways (taxi-out +3.9 min, higher than 86% of US airports), not at the gates (taxi-in -0.3 min, higher than only 7%).
  - A terminal-only project may not relieve its main bottleneck.
- **ORH moves above PWM.** Treat this with care:
  - ORH's C rests on only two of four signals, both taxi signals are missing, and its zero-width range reflects that gap rather than certainty.
  - ORH is also tiny, at 24K extra passengers.
  - PWM offers a larger opportunity (293K extra passengers) and shows a direct gate-wait signal (taxi-in +2.0 min, higher than 80%). Its range (10.5–11.3) overlaps BTV's at the low end.
- **BDL is the volume play.** It adds 580K extra passengers, the second-largest increase in the region. Its gate-wait signal is moderate (+1.3 min, higher than 70%).
- **MVY climbs on growth alone.** It has the region's highest growth forecast (63.7%), but its constraint is low (C 0.13). It can likely absorb much of that growth without expansion.
- **MHT slips to 9th** because its 10-year growth (13.0%) trails its peers.
- **Listed but not ranked** (no demand growth forecast):
  - Portsmouth/Pease (PSM) still has the highest constraint score in the data (C 0.73, peak saturation 100.0%). It is worth a separate look if a growth view can be formed.
  - Presque Isle (PQI): C 0.43.
  - Cape Cod Gateway (HYA): C 0.04.

**What this means for an investor:** a longer horizon strengthens the case for BOS on scale. BTV and PWM remain the cleaner *terminal* (gate-shortage) stories, and BDL offers meaningful absolute volume.

This is a screening signal, not investment advice.

### Assumptions & uncertainty

- **Data period and sources:**
  - Constraint signals cover Aug 2025 – Jul 2026, from BTS on-time data (domestic flights of reporting airlines only).
  - Growth is the FAA Terminal Area Forecast (TAF 2025) for FY2026–FY2036, a 10-year horizon. Forecasts can be wrong, and longer horizons add forecast risk.
- **What the score is:**
  - RPS = 100 × forecast growth × C.
  - RPS is a ranking index, not a passenger forecast.
  - It excludes construction cost, so it is not an IRR.
  - C ranks constraint relative to other US airports.
- **Ranges:** Ranges reflect only constraint uncertainty. Every airport carries the caveat that the growth range is not yet available, so true uncertainty is wider than shown.
- **Measurement limits:**
  - Demand turned away today can't be counted from public data. Full planes count only through load factor.
  - Gate shortage is inferred indirectly from planes waiting for a gate after landing.
- **Missing signals:**
  - ORH, MVY, PSM and PQI had 0 qualifying quiet-hour flights for both taxi-out and taxi-in (at least 30 are needed).
  - HYA had only 27 busy-hour flights for taxi-out and 0 quiet-hour flights for taxi-in.
  - For these airports, C is the average of the remaining signals.
- **Unforecast airports:** Airports the FAA doesn't forecast use a fallback growth of 2% a year. PSM, PQI and HYA have no demand growth forecast and are listed for information only, not ranked.
- **Scope notes:** None provided.
