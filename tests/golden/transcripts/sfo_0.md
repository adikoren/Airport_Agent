## Q: What is the unmet flight demand in SFO airport and why?

### Short answer

**SFO's unmet flight demand can't be stated as a number.** Public data cannot count demand that is turned away today. Instead, the data shows SFO as **heavily constrained, with strong growth ahead**:

- **Renovation Profitability Score (RPS): 10.6** (range 10.2–11.0)
- **Constraint score (C): 0.70** (range 0.67–0.73), which ranks how constrained SFO is compared with other US airports
- **Forecast growth: 15.2%** over FY2026–FY2031 (FAA TAF), or about **4.03M extra passengers** on top of **26.49M** in the last 12 months

In short, demand is projected to keep rising at an airport that already shows clear signs of running near its limits. Full planes are the closest measurable sign of excess demand.

### Why: the main drivers

| Constraint signal | SFO value | Compared with US airports |
|---|---|---|
| Cleaned taxi-out (runway queue) | +6.9 min | higher than 98% |
| Load factor | 82.1% | higher than 91% |
| Peak saturation | 88.7% | higher than 44% |
| Cleaned taxi-in (waiting for a gate) | +0.7 min | higher than 47% |

- **The runway is the bottleneck.** Extra taxi-out time is higher than at 98% of US airports. Departing planes queue for the runway more than almost anywhere else.
- **Planes are full.** A load factor of 82.1% is higher than at 91% of airports. Airlines are filling the seats they can schedule, which suggests some travelers are being priced out or turned away.
- **Gates and peak hours are not the main problem.** Peak saturation and gate-wait time are both near the middle of the US range. Measured indirectly, gate shortage does not look like SFO's binding constraint.
- **International traffic is not reflected in these signals.** SFO's international share is 28.1%, but the constraint signals cover domestic flights only.

### What this means for a renovation investment

SFO's constraint looks **mainly airside, centered on the runway**, rather than at terminals or gates. A terminal or gate renovation on its own may not unlock much added flight capacity if runway throughput stays the limit. The strong growth outlook and full planes do support demand for better passenger facilities. The clearest capacity upside, however, would come from anything that improves runway and airfield throughput. RPS does not include construction cost, so it is not a return estimate.

### Context from the web (unverified)

Web sources (news reports and an industry blog, not official FAA or airport sources) did not give a published figure for SFO's unmet demand. They point to these causes:

- **Runway layout.** SFO's two main landing runways are very closely spaced, which limits landings in low visibility.
- **Fog.** Fog and low ceilings often force reduced arrival rates, which leads to delays and cancellations.
- **FAA procedure changes.** The FAA reportedly ended side-by-side visual approaches to SFO's closely spaced runways, cutting landings even in clear weather. It later allowed staggered parallel approaches, which recovered some capacity.
- **Runway construction.** One runway was reportedly closed for a period and is said to be reopening early.

These accounts are consistent with the runway-queue signal above, but they are unverified.

### Assumptions & uncertainty

- **Data period:** Aug 2025 – Jul 2026 (12 months), with a 5-year forecast horizon.
- **Sources:** Growth comes from the FAA's Terminal Area Forecast (TAF 2025). Constraint signals come from BTS on-time data, covering domestic flights of reporting airlines only.
- **Ranges:** RPS 10.2–11.0 and C 0.67–0.73.
- **Caveat:** A growth range is not available yet. Only the constraint part of the range is measured.
- **Method:**
  - RPS = 100 × forecast growth × C. It is a ranking index, not a passenger forecast, and excludes construction cost, so it is not an IRR.
  - Forecasts can be wrong.
  - Airports the FAA doesn't forecast use a fallback growth of 2% a year. SFO uses the FAA TAF.
  - Demand turned away today can't be counted from public data. Full planes are captured through load factor.
  - Gate shortage is measured indirectly, from planes waiting for a gate after landing.
- **Scope notes:** None.

This is a screening signal, not investment advice.
