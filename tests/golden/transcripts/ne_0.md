## Q: Which airports in New England are strong candidates for terminal expansion?

### Strong candidates for terminal expansion in New England

**Top tier: Boston Logan (BOS) and Burlington (BTV).** Portland (PWM) and Worcester (ORH) form a second tier. Bradley (BDL) stands out for absolute volume even though its score is mid-pack.

### What drives the top of the list

- **BOS** combines strong forecast growth (13.8%) with broad congestion. Its load factor of 81.3% is higher than 87% of US airports, and its peak saturation is 92.5%. It also adds by far the most passengers in absolute terms (2.90M on a base of 20.95M), plus a meaningful international share (21.8%).
  - One caution for a *terminal* thesis: the pressure shows up mainly as runway queueing (taxi-out +3.9 min). Planes are not waiting for gates (taxi-in -0.3 min, higher than only 7% of airports). Gate capacity may therefore be less of a bottleneck than airside capacity.
- **BTV** has the highest constraint score among ranked airports (C 0.64). Its taxi-in delay is +3.3 min, higher than 93% of US airports, which is the clearest indirect sign of gate shortage in the region.
  - Its score range (6.9–8.3) overlaps BOS's, so the two are roughly tied on this metric.
  - The project scale is small: about 84K extra passengers on a 701K base.
- **PWM** pairs 12.9% growth with very full planes and some gate waiting (taxi-in +2.0 min, higher than 80%). Its runway queue is negligible (taxi-out -2.1 min).
- **ORH** is fully saturated at peak (100.0%). However, it is very small (13K extra passengers), and two of its four constraint signals are missing, so its C rests on only two signals.
- **BDL** has a moderate score, but its 321K extra passengers are the second-largest absolute increase. That matters for the size of the investment.

### Watch-outs lower down

- **MVY** has the highest growth forecast (32.4%), but low constraint (C 0.13). That suggests it can likely absorb the growth without major expansion.
- **ACK** shows strong gate-wait signals (taxi-in +3.9 min, higher than 95%), but growth is weak (6.8%).
- **Listed but not ranked** (no FAA demand growth forecast):
  - **Portsmouth/Pease (PSM)** has the highest constraint score in the data (C 0.73), with 100.0% peak saturation. It may merit a separate look.
  - **Presque Isle (PQI)**: C 0.43.
  - **Cape Cod Gateway (HYA)**: C 0.04.

This is a screening signal, not investment advice.

### Assumptions & uncertainty

- **Data period and sources:**
  - Constraint data covers Aug 2025 – Jul 2026, from BTS on-time data (domestic flights of reporting airlines only).
  - Growth is the FAA Terminal Area Forecast (TAF 2025) for FY2026–FY2031, a 5-year horizon. Forecasts can be wrong.
- **How the score works:**
  - RPS = 100 × forecast growth × C.
  - RPS is a ranking index, not a passenger forecast.
  - RPS excludes construction cost, so it is not an IRR.
  - C ranks how constrained an airport is relative to other US airports.
- **Ranges:**
  - The RPS and C ranges shown reflect only constraint uncertainty.
  - Every airport carries the caveat that the growth range is not yet available.
- **Missing signals:**
  - ORH, MVY, PSM and PQI each had 0 qualifying quiet-hour flights for both taxi-out and taxi-in. At least 30 are needed for a reliable mean.
  - HYA had only 27 busy-hour flights for taxi-out and 0 quiet-hour flights for taxi-in.
  - For all of these airports, C is the average of the remaining signals.
- **Measurement limits:**
  - Demand turned away today cannot be counted from public data. Full planes are captured only through load factor.
  - Gate shortage is inferred indirectly from planes waiting for a gate after landing.
- **Unforecast airports:** Airports the FAA doesn't forecast would use a fallback growth of 2% a year. PSM, PQI and HYA show no growth forecast and are listed for information only, not ranked.
- **Scope notes:** None provided.
