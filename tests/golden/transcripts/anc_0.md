## Q: What is the percentage of long haul flights out of Anchorage airport?

### Answer: long-haul share at Anchorage (ANC)

**About 20.1% of ANC flights are long-haul when measured by block time, and 6.6% when measured by distance.** These figures come from the OAG schedule for 2026-10-15, covering 441 operating flights.

| Measure | All flights (441) | Passenger flights (106) |
|---|---|---|
| Long-haul by block time (≥ 6 hours gate to gate) | 20.1% | 6.6% |
| Long-haul by distance (≥ 3,000 miles) | 6.6% | 0.9% |
| International share of all flights | 13.6% | n/a |

### Why the two measures differ

- **Block time vs. distance:** Block time counts slow or long-routed flights as long-haul even when the distance is under 3,000 miles. That is why the block-time share is higher.
- **Missing block times:** 202 of the 441 flights had no usable block time. The distance-based share does cover these flights, so it is the more complete measure.
- **Cargo dominates:** Only 106 of the 441 flights are passenger flights. Long-haul activity is concentrated in the non-passenger (largely cargo) operations. Among passenger flights, long-haul is small: 6.6% by block time and 0.9% by distance.

### What this means for a renovation investment

ANC's long-haul and international profile mostly reflects its role as a freight and transit hub, not passenger demand. For a passenger-terminal renovation, long-haul passenger traffic (for example, widebody gates or international processing) looks like a minor driver. Passenger flights are overwhelmingly short- to medium-haul. Any case for long-haul-oriented passenger facilities would need support beyond this schedule snapshot. Cargo-related infrastructure is where the long-haul activity sits.

This is a screening signal, not investment advice.

### Assumptions & uncertainty

- **Data period and source:** The long-haul figures come from the OAG schedule for a single day (2026-10-15). They count operating flights only, with codeshare copies removed, and include both domestic and international flights. One day may not reflect seasonal variation. The broader scoring framework (Renovation Profitability Score, RPS) uses a data period of Aug 2025 – Jul 2026. No forecast horizon applies to this metric.
- **Definitions:** A flight is long-haul if its scheduled block time is 6 hours or more, or if its distance is 3,000 miles or more.
- **Ranges:** The long-haul share is 6.6%–20.1% for all flights and 0.9%–6.6% for passenger flights, depending on the definition.
- **Caveats:**
  - 202 flights had no usable block time. The distance-based share covers them.
  - The data is an OAG schedule for one day, operating flights only (codeshare copies removed), domestic and international.
- **Framework assumptions (RPS, not used directly here):**
  - Growth is based on the FAA Terminal Area Forecast, which can be wrong. Airports the FAA doesn't forecast use a 2% a year fallback.
  - C ranks how constrained an airport is compared with other US airports. RPS is a ranking index, not a passenger forecast.
  - Demand turned away today can't be counted from public data. Full planes count through load factor.
  - Gate shortage is inferred from planes waiting for a gate after landing.
  - Constraint signals use BTS on-time data, covering domestic flights of reporting airlines only.
  - RPS excludes construction cost and is not an IRR.
- **Scope notes:** None provided.
