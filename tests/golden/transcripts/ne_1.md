## Q: Why is the first one first?

### Why Boston Logan (BOS) ranks first

**BOS ranks first because it has the strongest combination of forecast growth and congestion, though its lead over Burlington (BTV) is not statistically secure.** The score is RPS = 100 × forecast growth × C. BOS scores 8.1, ahead of BTV at 7.6 and Portland (PWM) at 6.1.

| Airport | Forecast growth FY2026–FY2031 | C (range) | RPS (range) |
|---|---|---|---|
| BOS | 13.8% | 0.59 (0.56–0.59) | 8.1 (7.8–8.2) |
| BTV | 12.0% | 0.64 (0.57–0.69) | 7.6 (6.9–8.3) |
| PWM | 12.9% | 0.47 (0.46–0.49) | 6.1 (5.9–6.4) |

### The two drivers

- **Growth carries BOS past BTV.** BTV is actually more constrained (C 0.64 vs 0.59). BOS wins because its higher growth forecast (13.8% vs 12.0%) outweighs that gap in the multiplication.
- **Congestion is broad, not one-dimensional.** Three of BOS's four signals sit at or above the US median:
  - Load factor is 81.3%, higher than 87% of US airports.
  - Runway queueing (taxi-out) is +3.9 min, higher than 86%.
  - Peak saturation is 92.5%, higher than 54%.
- **Scale is not part of the score, but it matters to an investor.** BOS adds 2.90M passengers on a base of 20.95M, versus 84K for BTV. It also has a 21.8% international share. RPS uses percentage growth, so this volume advantage is a separate argument in BOS's favour.

### What this means for a terminal investment

- **The #1 spot is fragile.** BTV's RPS range (6.9–8.3) overlaps BOS's (7.8–8.2), so the two are effectively tied. PWM is clearly behind.
- **BOS's pressure is mainly airside, not at the gates.** Taxi-in, the gate-shortage proxy, is -0.3 min, higher than only 7% of US airports. Planes are queueing for runways, not waiting for gates.
  - A terminal-only expansion at BOS may therefore address the wrong bottleneck.
  - BTV's taxi-in of +3.3 min (higher than 93%) is a more direct gate-shortage signal.

This is a screening signal, not investment advice.

### Assumptions & uncertainty

- **Data period and sources:**
  - Constraint signals cover Aug 2025 – Jul 2026, from BTS on-time data (domestic flights of reporting airlines only).
  - Growth is the FAA Terminal Area Forecast (TAF 2025) for FY2026–FY2031, a 5-year horizon. Forecasts can be wrong.
- **What the score is:**
  - RPS is a ranking index, not a passenger forecast.
  - It excludes construction cost, so it is not an IRR.
  - C ranks constraint relative to other US airports.
- **Ranges:** Ranges reflect only constraint uncertainty. Every airport carries the caveat that the growth range is not yet available, so true uncertainty is wider than shown.
- **Measurement limits:**
  - Demand turned away today can't be counted from public data. Full planes are captured only through load factor.
  - Gate shortage is inferred indirectly from planes waiting for a gate after landing.
- **Missing signals:**
  - ORH, MVY, PSM and PQI had 0 qualifying quiet-hour flights for both taxi-out and taxi-in (at least 30 are needed).
  - HYA had only 27 busy-hour flights for taxi-out and 0 quiet-hour flights for taxi-in.
  - For these airports, C is the average of the remaining signals. BOS and BTV have all four signals.
- **Unforecast airports:** Airports the FAA doesn't forecast use a fallback growth of 2% a year. PSM, PQI and HYA have no growth forecast and are listed for information only, not ranked.
